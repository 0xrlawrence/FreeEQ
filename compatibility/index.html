What works on your phone

No Android app can equalize everything. This is what FreeEQ reaches, what it needs from you, and what nobody can process, so you know before you install.

Apps

FreeEQ attaches to each app’s audio as it starts. Inside the app, a status strip shows which apps are being processed right now and how to fix the rest.

The lists below describe how each player works with Android’s audio effects, as other EQ apps have documented it. A player update can change that, which is why the app checks every app as it plays instead of trusting the list.

On some phones FreeEQ can also process everything at once (global mode). It checks that with a short listening test first, because it fails on some setups, for example Pixel phones streaming LDAC or aptX.

For exact filters there is Studio mode, which re-records other apps’ sound. Android asks for consent when it starts and shows its screen-cast indicator, and Studio mode adds a short delay, so it stays optional. Spotify and Chrome forbid that recording, so they stay on the normal per-app mode, which still works.

No app can process it

Some audio skips every effect on Android. FreeEQ shows these cases instead of pretending to process them.

Low-latency game audio
Games that use Android’s fastest audio paths (AAudio “MMAP” and “fast” tracks) skip every audio effect, so no EQ app can reach them.
Bit-perfect USB DAC playback
Bit-perfect mode sends the music to a USB DAC untouched; that is the point of it. Turn it off in the player to use FreeEQ, or let FreeEQ write the EQ into a supported USB-C dongle instead.
Hi-res “direct” output in some players
Direct and hi-res output options bypass Android’s effects. Switch them off in the player (in VLC, set Audio output to AudioTrack; in Poweramp, turn off Hi-Res output).
Dolby Atmos spatial tracks
Apple Music plays its Dolby Atmos tracks through a path that skips app EQ. Its lossless stereo tracks work.

Phone brands

Phone makers ship their own sound engines, which add EQ on top of FreeEQ or get silenced by it. FreeEQ’s Audio Path Doctor finds the known ones on your phone and links to the switch.

Google Pixel

What works

  • Per-app mode works with players that announce themselves (Spotify, YouTube Music and others).
  • No Dolby- or Dirac-style music engine: the cleanest base for correction.

What gets in the way

  • Spatial Audio changes the sound with a head model and limits which audio the global mode reaches.
  • Global mode: LDAC and aptX streams skipped the global effect on Pixel 6/7/8a while SBC and AAC worked; on a Pixel 10a with Android 17 global mode had no effect at all.
  • Adaptive Sound only tunes the phone speaker, not headphones.

For the most accurate sound

  1. Open your earbuds’ Bluetooth settings and turn off Spatial Audio / head tracking for the most accurate sound.
  2. Close or uninstall other equalizer apps (only one app can control the EQ at a time).

Before the one-time unlock

  • Android 17 broke app detection on some Pixels (7 Pro, 8a): the player’s audio was found but no EQ was attached (Wavelet reports). If a player stays unprocessed after the unlock, play your music in FreeEQ’s own player.

Samsung Galaxy

Built-in sound engines: Dolby Atmos (Dolby Audio Processing), Samsung SoundAlive (Equalizer), Samsung MySpace (sa3d), Samsung SoundBooster+ and volume monitor, Dolby game, voice and spatial helpers.

What works

  • Per-app mode works; global mode worked on a Galaxy S23 Ultra.
  • On Galaxy phones with Exynos or Snapdragon chips, Galaxy Buds can use Samsung’s own codec (SSC).

What gets in the way

  • Dolby Atmos, the One UI Equalizer and Adapt Sound all add their own EQ on top of FreeEQ.
  • The UHQ upscaler can cause clipping with third-party EQ (one developer’s report).
  • With Galaxy Buds connected, 360 Audio replaces the Dolby Atmos menu.
  • A user reported the built-in equalizer stopped working once another EQ app was installed.

For the most accurate sound

  1. Turn off Dolby Atmos (quick-settings tile or Sound quality and effects).
  2. Set the Equalizer to Normal.
  3. Turn off Adapt Sound.
  4. If you hear crackling, set UHQ upscaler to "Bit upscaling only".
  5. Turn off 360 Audio for Galaxy Buds.
  6. Remove Sound Assistant’s equalizer if you use it.

Xiaomi, Redmi and POCO

Built-in sound engines: Dolby Atmos (Dolby Audio Processing), Xiaomi Mi Sound, Dirac, Dolby game, voice and spatial helpers, Qualcomm offload effects (bass boost, virtualizer, EQ).

What works

  • Per-app mode works; the global mode worked on a Xiaomi 17 Ultra and a Redmi Note 13.

What gets in the way

  • Dolby Atmos (newer models), Mi Sound headphone presets and Dirac (older models) stack with FreeEQ.
  • MediaTek-based models may also run MediaTek’s loudness processing inside the audio hardware layer, which no app can switch off.
  • One HyperOS build for the POCO F7 (OS3.0.5.0.WOJMIXM) had a Dolby audio bug and was pulled (third-party report).

For the most accurate sound

  1. Turn off Dolby Atmos.
  2. Open Sound settings > Earphones and turn off the headphone-type preset and personalised sound.
  3. Close or uninstall other equalizer apps (only one app can control the EQ at a time).

Before the one-time unlock

  • In Developer options, also turn on "USB debugging (Security settings)" before the one-time unlock.

Other phones

What works

  • Per-app mode works with players that announce themselves; the global mode depends on the phone and is checked by a listening test.

What gets in the way

  • Any built-in "sound effects", Dolby or spatial audio stacks with FreeEQ.
  • Hi-res "direct" output, USB DAC bit-perfect mode, Dolby Atmos passthrough and low-latency game audio bypass every app EQ.
  • If the global mode does nothing over Bluetooth, the Developer option "Disable Bluetooth A2DP hardware offload" may be on.

For the most accurate sound

  1. Turn off the phone’s own sound effects, Dolby or equalizer.
  2. Open your earbuds’ Bluetooth settings and turn off Spatial Audio / head tracking for the most accurate sound.
  3. If you hear no difference over Bluetooth or a USB DAC, try setting the Bluetooth sample rate to 48 kHz or the codec to SBC in Developer options.
  4. In music players with a hi-res or bit-perfect output option, switch it off (e.g. VLC: Audio output = AudioTrack).
  5. Close or uninstall other equalizer apps (only one app can control the EQ at a time).

Qualcomm Snapdragon

Snapdragon phones have the widest codec choice. aptX Adaptive and aptX Lossless work only if your phone maker enabled them and your earbuds have a Qualcomm chip.

  • aptX Adaptive needs Snapdragon 855 or newer and the maker’s support; Android does not report it by a standard number.
  • aptX Lossless needs a Snapdragon Sound certified phone and earbuds.
  • LC3 (LE Audio) needs Android 13 or newer and a phone whose maker turned it on; most 2024 and newer flagships have it.
  • Samsung Galaxy phones with Snapdragon use Samsung’s codec set instead (see the Samsung entry).

For example: Xiaomi 15 (SBC, AAC, aptX, aptX HD, aptX Adaptive, LDAC, LHDC 1.0–5.0, LC3); Honor Magic series; Motorola, OnePlus, vivo/iQOO, Nothing.

MediaTek Dimensity / Helio

Your MediaTek phone can’t do aptX Adaptive or aptX Lossless, even if your earbuds can. LDAC (660 kbps is the stable setting) is your best option, or LHDC if your phone offers it.

  • realme confirms MediaTek models cannot select aptX Adaptive "due to the limitations of the MTK processor platform".
  • Some makers leave out aptX entirely: the Xiaomi 14T Pro lists AAC, LDAC, LHDC 4.0 and LC3 only.
  • vivo worked with MediaTek to bring LHDC lossless (up to 1.4 Mbps) to Dimensity phones.
  • LHDC and LC3 (LE Audio) are on Dimensity phones whose makers ship them, such as the Xiaomi 14T Pro; budget Helio phones often have neither.
  • On Samsung Galaxy A models with MediaTek chips, Samsung picks the codecs. Its Exynos and Snapdragon Galaxies get SBC, AAC, aptX, LDAC and Samsung’s own codec (SSC), but nobody has published the list for the MediaTek models, so trust the codecs your phone reports.

For example: Xiaomi 14T Pro (Dimensity 9300+); vivo X200/X300; OPPO Find X; realme, Infinix, Tecno; Samsung Galaxy A models with MediaTek chips.

Samsung Galaxy

On Galaxy phones with Exynos or Snapdragon chips, Samsung decides the codecs, not the chip: SBC, AAC, aptX, LDAC and Samsung’s own codec (SSC) with Galaxy Buds, but not aptX HD, aptX Adaptive or LHDC, even on Snapdragon models.

  • Samsung’s press claim that the Galaxy S25 adds aptX HD/Adaptive and LHDC was corrected: it has the same codecs as earlier models.
  • SSC UHQ (24-bit/96 kHz) needs Galaxy Buds3 Pro or Buds4 Pro and the "Ultra High Quality Audio" setting.
  • LE Audio (LC3) arrived with One UI 5.1 and Auracast with One UI 7, on models whose hardware supports it.
  • This codec set is confirmed for Exynos and Snapdragon Galaxies only. Galaxy A models with MediaTek or Unisoc chips are listed under those chips, because nobody has published their exact list.
  • Exynos phones from other makers follow that maker’s codec list, not Samsung’s.

For example: Galaxy S and Z series; Galaxy A models with Exynos or Snapdragon chips.

Google Pixel

Pixels support LDAC, Opus with Pixel Buds Pro and, from Pixel 7, LC3 (LE Audio); Android 17 added LHDC v5 on Pixel 9 Pro and newer. aptX Adaptive is not available.

  • LHDC v5 (up to 96 kHz) appears under Developer options > Bluetooth audio codec only while an LHDC v5 headset is connected.
  • LE Audio arrived with Pixel 7 / Android 13; Auracast audio sharing with Android 16 QPR1 on Pixel 8 and newer (not 8a/9a).
  • Reports about aptX and aptX HD on the Pixel 9 versus the 9a conflict.

For example: Pixel 6–10 series (Google Tensor chips).

Unisoc (budget phones)

On budget Unisoc phones, expect SBC or AAC. EQ is the biggest improvement you can make.

  • The exact codec list of Unisoc phones is not documented publicly; it likely follows standard Android (SBC, AAC, maybe LDAC and aptX).
  • This includes budget Samsung Galaxy A0x models: Samsung has not published which codecs they get.

For example: Budget Samsung A0x, Tecno, Infinix, Nokia and ZTE models.

Huawei Kirin

Huawei phones support LDAC, LHDC and, with Huawei FreeBuds, L2HC. Phones on HarmonyOS NEXT cannot run Android apps like FreeEQ.

  • L2HC 4.0 needs EMUI 15 or newer.
  • LLAC (low-latency LHDC) has been on Huawei phones since the P30.

For example: Huawei Mate, Pura and nova series.

Earbud chips

Earbuds rarely say which chip they use. This is where each chip family is usually found and what it can decode.

JieLiAC69xx, AC70xx, JL70xx
The largest share of no-name $1–$15 wireless earbuds (about 26% of all TWS main chips in 2022). SBC and AAC (mSBC for calls). The JL7018 appears with LDAC in the FiiO EH11, although its datasheet lists SBC/AAC.
Expect SBC (maybe AAC) and roughly 150–200 ms of delay. There is no hi-res option, so EQ is the one real quality lever.
BluetrumAB53xx, AB563x, AB571x, AB573x, BT891x, BT892x, BT897x
The second-largest white-label TWS chip maker (about 20% in 2022), historically the cheapest chips. Entry AB573x: no hi-res codec. BT891x: LDAC. BT892x: AAC, LHDC, LDAC. BT897x: LE Audio plus LDAC/LHDC.
Many of these chips have a built-in "bass enhancement" engine. Turn off the earbuds’ own bass or EQ mode first ("flatten your buds"), then let FreeEQ correct them.
Bestechnic (BES)BES2300, BES2500, BES2600, BES2700, BES2800
Mid-range buds from Huawei, Xiaomi/Redmi, OPPO, Motorola and similar brands ($20–$200). LHDC on BES2500Y (OPPO Enco X) and reportedly BES2600/2700 (Moto Buds). BES2800 codec list unconfirmed.
Often offers an LHDC or LDAC option next to SBC/AAC; check your earbuds’ app.
AirohaAB156x, AB158x, AB1595
Mid-tier brands, and some white-label earbuds. LE Audio on AB1565/AB1568/AB1585. LDAC is not confirmed by any datasheet found.
Probably SBC, AAC and LC3; some designs add LDAC.
RealtekRTL8763, RTL8773
Budget to mid-range earbuds (teardown reports name SoundPeats/QCY-class models; unverified per model). RTL8763E/RTL8773E: dual-mode classic Bluetooth plus LE Audio.
Treat as SBC/AAC, plus LC3 on newer parts, unless your phone reports otherwise.
ActionsATS30xx, ATS308x, ATS296x
Smartwatches and some budget audio products. ATS308x: watch chip with LE Audio. ATS296X: announced Bluetooth HDT support.
Treat as SBC/AAC (plus LC3 on newer parts) unless your phone reports otherwise.
Qualcomm QCCQCC304x, QCC305x, QCC514x, QCC515x, QCC3071, QCC5171, QCC3091, S3/S5 Gen 3, S7 / S7 Pro
Mid and premium earbuds (Jabra, Nothing, Bose, Xiaomi Buds 5 Pro, older Sony). Usually $40 and up. aptX, aptX HD and aptX Adaptive (QCC304x and newer); aptX Lossless (QCC5171/3071/3091); LE Audio (QCC3071 and newer); Wi-Fi audio (QCC7226).
With a Snapdragon phone you may get aptX Adaptive or Lossless; with other phones it falls back to AAC, SBC or LDAC.
