Privacy

FreeEQ itself collects no personal data. There is no account, no advertising and no analytics, in the app or on this website.

Last updated 3 November 2026.

The app

Your profiles, settings, earbud names and listening stay on your phone. The headphone database is built into the app, so finding your earphones and applying a correction works offline.

FreeEQ sends no information about you or your listening anywhere. It has no analytics, crash reporting or advertising code. It goes online only when you ask it to check for newer headphone data, and links to guides open in your browser.

Optional contributions

The in-app contribution is optional and unlocks nothing. Nothing contacts RevenueCat unless you tap to contribute. If you do, Google Play takes the payment and RevenueCat records the purchase using an anonymous ID, the product bought and basic app and device details. FreeEQ receives no payment card details. See the Google and RevenueCat privacy policies.

Ko-fi and Ethereum donations happen outside the app, under those services’ own terms. An Ethereum transaction is public on the blockchain.

Why the app asks for permissions

Nearby devices (Bluetooth)
To read your earbuds’ name, codec and battery level and to switch to the right profile when they connect. This information stays on your phone.
Notifications
Android requires a notification while the audio engine runs in the background. It also holds the profile switcher.
Music and audio files
Only for the built-in player, to list and play the music already on your phone.
Microphone (record audio)
Only for the optional Studio mode. Android files the re-routing of other apps’ sound under this permission. FreeEQ processes that sound live and never saves or sends it. It does not use the microphone.
The one-time unlock
Granted once, through Wireless debugging on the phone, the free Shizuku app or a computer. It lets FreeEQ read Android’s audio and Bluetooth status: which apps are playing, so it can attach the EQ to them, and details such as the active codec and each earbud’s battery. That information stays on your phone.
Companion device association
An optional one-tap system dialog per pair of earbuds, so FreeEQ can start when they connect and, on Android 14 and newer, change the Bluetooth codec.
USB devices
Android asks once per USB-C dongle, and only when you choose to save the EQ into a dongle that has its own EQ chip.
Start after a restart
If you turn on auto-start, FreeEQ starts again after your phone restarts. Android 15 and newer wait until your headphones connect or you open the app.
Internet
Used only when you tap “Check for newer data” under Headphone data in Settings. FreeEQ then downloads a small public file from freeeq.app to compare versions, and sends nothing about you.

This website

This site uses no cookies, analytics, trackers or third-party scripts. Its font is served from this site too.

The headphone search downloads the public list of measurements and searches it in your browser. What you type is never sent anywhere. Copying an export or saving it as a file happens in your browser as well.

The web host may keep standard access logs (such as IP address, time and page requested) to keep the site running. FreeEQ adds no tracking on top of that.

Share links

A FreeEQ share link holds the EQ itself after the “#” in the address, for example freeeq.app/p/#…. Browsers never send that part of an address to a server, so opening a share link tells nobody, us included, which EQ you opened. Links contain the profile’s name and settings, not who made them.
