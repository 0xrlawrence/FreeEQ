    Questions people ask.

    Is it really free?
    Yes. FreeEQ has no paid version, no ads and no account, and its code is open source under the GNU GPL 3.0 or later. The AutoEq corrections, the Sound Twin, the Taste test and the Fit Coach are all included.
    My earphones are not in the search. Can FreeEQ still help?
    Yes, that is the main thing it was built for. Sound Twin asks what kind of earphones you have, roughly what they cost, what sounds wrong and how they fit, then applies the correction for the closest group of measured budget models. The Taste test and Fit Coach refine it by ear. How each one works
    Does it work with YouTube, Spotify and my other apps?
    Spotify, YouTube Music, Samsung Music and many players work with no setup. YouTube, TIDAL, Amazon Music and other apps that never announce themselves work after a one-time unlock that takes about two minutes. Some audio, like low-latency game sound, can’t be processed by any app. See the full list
    Do I need root?
    No. The one-time unlock is done with the free Shizuku app on the phone itself, or from a computer with a USB cable. It lets FreeEQ see which app is playing so it can attach the EQ, and it keeps working after a restart.
    Can it make my Bluetooth earbuds use aptX or LDAC?
    No app can add a codec: the phone and the earbuds both have to support it, and the cheapest earbud chips only decode SBC and AAC. FreeEQ shapes the sound before the phone compresses it for Bluetooth, so it works with every codec, and it shows you which codec is in use and which ones your phone could use.
    Will it hurt my earbuds or my ears?
    FreeEQ always lowers the overall level a little before boosting anything (a preamp), runs a limiter so peaks cannot clip, and caps boosts at +6 dB by default to protect small drivers. It is a sound tool, not a medical one, and makes no claims about hearing.
    Does it drain the battery?
    A little, while music plays: an active EQ keeps the phone off its most power-efficient audio path. To keep that small, FreeEQ releases its effect when nothing has played for a while and attaches again when playback starts.
    Why is there a slight delay on some phones?
    Android’s built-in equalizer works in short blocks of sound. Longer blocks fix deep bass more precisely but add delay, so FreeEQ uses longer blocks for music and shorter ones for video, automatically. You can choose the trade-off yourself in the app.
    What does the score mean?
    It is the Harman preference score that AutoEq uses: a prediction, from Harman’s listening research, of how much people like a headphone’s sound, before and after correction. It only works for in-ears measured on an IEC 711 or GRAS RA0045 coupler and over-ears on a GRAS 43AG-7, so FreeEQ hides it for everything else rather than showing a made-up number.
    Does it work with USB-C dongles?
    Yes. Wired earphones on a USB-C dongle work like any other output. Some dongles with a built-in EQ chip (models from FiiO, Moondrop, Qudelix, JDS Labs and WalkPlay- or KT Micro-based adapters) can also store the EQ in the dongle itself, so it works in every app with no delay.
    Is there an iPhone version?
    No. FreeEQ is for Android 9 and newer. iPhones do not let apps equalize other apps’ audio.
    Where does the headphone data come from?
    From AutoEq, Jaakko Pasanen’s open project, which turns measurements by independent reviewers into corrections. Every result shows who measured it. All sources and licences
