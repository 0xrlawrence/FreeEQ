Credits

FreeEQ stands on other people’s work: an open-source correction engine, thousands of careful measurements by independent reviewers, and years of listening research. FreeEQ itself is free software under the GNU GPL 3.0 or later.

The measurements

Every correction comes from AutoEq’s published results (8,850 measurements of 4,340 models, AutoEq data of 20 July 2025). AutoEq’s code is MIT-licensed; the measurements belong to the reviewers below. FreeEQ uses the published results free of charge, names the reviewer next to every result, and never includes crinacle’s raw measurements, at his request.

Code FreeEQ learned from

Open-source projects whose code or methods FreeEQ adapts, each credited in the source files.

AutoEq
by Jaakko Pasanen. FreeEQ’s EQ maths is a TypeScript port of its correction, filter fitting and preference score, and its database results are the corrections you search.
MIT
Equalizer314
The approach to Android’s DynamicsProcessing engine (band layout read-back, a compatibility mode for Pixel phones) follows this open-source equalizer.
GPL-3.0
RootlessJamesDSP
by Tim Schneeberger. Studio mode’s capture setup and the JamesDSP export formats follow it.
GPL-3.0
PublicGraphTool (devicePEQ)
The protocols for writing EQ into USB-C dongles from FiiO, Moondrop, Qudelix, JDS Labs, WalkPlay and KT Micro come from its device code.
MIT
EqualizerAPO
by Jonas Thedering. Its filter and GraphicEQ rules are re-implemented so FreeEQ reads and writes its files exactly.
GPL-2.0-or-later

Research

  • Harman’s headphone preference research (Olive and colleagues): the target curves and the preference score.
  • ISO 226:2023 equal-loudness contours: volume-dependent loudness compensation.
  • ITU-R BS.1770: loudness matching in the Taste test.
  • Hunter (2004) and Davidson (1970): the paired-comparison statistics behind the Taste test.
